Project notes / Beauty

What the brands
signal.

A small desk-research exercise using official brand websites. This is a qualitative positioning comparison, not a sales, pricing or campaign-performance audit.

Brand 01 · L’Oréal Paris

Accessible beauty with science and empowerment

The UK brand site emphasises accessible beauty, product efficacy, scientific innovation and women’s empowerment. Its long-running “I’m worth it” platform gives the brand an established emotional message to connect product benefits with confidence and self-expression. Official brand overview ↗

Brand 02 · Maybelline New York

Trend-led, practical, high-performance makeup

Maybelline’s UK positioning highlights New York trend cues, makeup for different looks and skin tones, convenience and performance. Its product pages make hero products and shade choices easy to explore. Official brand overview ↗

Brand 03 · e.l.f. Cosmetics

Accessible price, inclusivity and community

e.l.f.’s UK site foregrounds accessibility, everyday value, vegan and cruelty-free claims, and feedback from its community. This provides a clear contrast in value-led messaging and community-oriented brand language. Official brand overview ↗

Opportunity · UK audience

Turn product proof into relatable content

Recommendation: test short-form creator demonstrations that pair a clear product benefit with real-life UK routines, varied skin tones and concise proof points. Keep the creative premium but approachable, and localise language, seasonal hooks and retail calls to action for the UK audience.

Measurement plan

Track attention, intent and efficiency

For a proposed campaign, compare video completion, saves/shares, click-through rate and product-page visits by creative variation. For a pricing audit, record the same product size/shade, UK RRP, promotional price, retailer and date captured; do not compare mismatched pack sizes or temporary offers.

Scope and accuracy: Prepared as an independent portfolio exercise from official brand websites, accessed October 2026. Brand descriptions are qualitative summaries; the campaign idea and measurement plan are recommendations, not results from a live campaign. No prices or performance figures have been invented.
